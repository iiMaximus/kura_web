Direct product data

Two open databases, different product categories.

Open Food FactsFood barcode and product-label data. Collaborative records can be incomplete, outdated or different from the package in your hand.Open Beauty FactsCosmetic barcode and product-label data. Product coverage and ingredient completeness vary.

Contributed records can contain errors or incomplete fields. Formulas and regional versions may differ. Check the current packaging, and review the database’s attribution and licensing information when reusing its data.

Evidence boundaries

Keep a label, an estimate and a test separate.

An ingredient list does not report heavy-metal or microplastic concentrations. A category-based estimate is not a measurement. A published lab test relates to the tested product and sample; it should not be extended to every formula, batch or similar product.

Lychee’s methodology explains how incomplete inputs and ingredient coverage affect results.

Common questions

Before you scan.

Where does Lychee get product data?

Food barcode lookup uses Open Food Facts; cosmetic barcode lookup uses Open Beauty Facts. Label photos use OCR. Lychee applies its own scoring logic and ingredient dictionaries to available information.

Does Lychee use AI to calculate every score?

The food and cosmetic scores use scoring logic and ingredient dictionaries. Optional AI assistance adds explanations and analysis; it is not a laboratory test or a guarantee that a label is accurate.

Can Lychee detect heavy metals or microplastics?

A barcode or label photo cannot measure contaminants. Where Lychee presents estimates or published lab results, those are different forms of evidence. Check the source, tested product, date and limits before drawing a conclusion.
