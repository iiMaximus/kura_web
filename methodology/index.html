Scoring methodology

A useful score needs reasons and limits.

Food scoring

Processing comes first. Nutrition still matters.

Lychee’s food Health Score runs from 0 to 100. Whole and minimally processed foods should generally score higher than heavily engineered products. A good-looking macro profile should not erase evidence of ultra-processing.

Processing

NOVA data and ingredient-list signals help establish the starting point. Label evidence can challenge an incomplete or inconsistent database classification.

Ingredients and additives

Additives, industrial formulation markers, seed oils and ingredient quality can affect the result. Front-of-pack health claims do not override the ingredient list.

Nutrition

Sugar, salt, saturated fat, fiber and protein are considered alongside processing. Lychee displays available nutrition, including energy; it is not a calorie-tracking program.

There is no single fixed percentage formula for every product. Category-specific rules, penalties, caps and data checks are part of the model. NOVA and Nutri-Score provide context; neither is a complete substitute for Lychee’s score.

A high food score does not override an allergen alert. A low score is not a diagnosis. Compare products used for similar purposes and read the reasons, not just the number.

Some products do not receive a food score. Insufficient information, supplements requiring dose-specific context, and pure water are examples where a comparative number may not be useful.

Cosmetic scoring · beta

The formula and how you use it both matter.

Cosmetics use a separate model. Lychee recognizes INCI ingredients, explains their functions and concern categories, and considers product-use context such as rinse-off, leave-on, lip, oral, eye-area and fragrance exposure.

  • Ingredient coverage is checked before a score appears. Recognized and unknown ingredients remain useful information even when no score is available.
  • The general coverage gate requires at least three ingredients and 75% recognition. Additional checks can withhold a score for a short OCR extract, mismatched product data or poor confidence; selected single-ingredient formulas are an exception.
  • When ingredient coverage is strong but product type is uncertain, Lychee can use a cautious exposure assumption instead of automatically blocking the score.
  • Concentration, the finished formulation and personal sensitivity cannot be fully determined from an INCI list. A flagged ingredient is a reason to read more, not proof of harm.

Not enough data is a valid result

Low coverage should not produce a reassuring number. The cosmetic score is beta, and ingredient explanations remain visible so you can inspect the label yourself.

Data and interpretation

Keep the inputs and interpretation separate.

Product label

Barcode records come from open product databases or text read from a label. Neither proves that the current package has the same formula.

Scoring model

Lychee applies its own rules to available inputs. The number summarizes those rules; it is not a regulator’s approval or a clinical assessment.

AI and estimates

AI explanations and contaminant estimates provide additional context. They cannot measure contamination or establish individual medical risk.

Published laboratory results, when present, should be read separately from estimates and tied to the specific tested product and source. Scoring evolves as data and implementation improve; old saved results may differ from a fresh analysis.

Common questions

Before you scan.

How does Lychee score food?

Lychee uses a 0–100 food score that prioritizes processing. Additives, ingredient quality, sugar, salt, saturated fat, fiber and protein also matter. NOVA and Nutri-Score are inputs, not the entire score. Missing data can reduce confidence or prevent a score.

Does Lychee scan cosmetics and skincare?

Yes. Lychee scans beauty and personal-care barcodes and ingredient labels. Its beta cosmetic score considers recognized ingredients, concerns and exposure, including rinse-off versus leave-on use. It shows Not enough data when ingredient coverage is insufficient.

Does Lychee use AI to calculate every score?

The food and cosmetic scores use scoring logic and ingredient dictionaries. Optional AI assistance adds explanations and analysis; it is not a laboratory test or a guarantee that a label is accurate.

Can Lychee detect heavy metals or microplastics?

A barcode or label photo cannot measure contaminants. Where Lychee presents estimates or published lab results, those are different forms of evidence. Check the source, tested product, date and limits before drawing a conclusion.
