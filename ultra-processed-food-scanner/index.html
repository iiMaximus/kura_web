Read processing in context

NOVA is a useful input, not the whole verdict.

NOVA groups foods by the nature and purpose of processing. Lychee uses available NOVA information and can estimate processing from ingredient signals when database information is absent and the evidence supports an estimate.

Ingredient names, formulations and product categories matter. The model has category-specific checks and can use label evidence to challenge an inconsistent catalog tag. Read the food scoring methodology for the approach.

When information is thin

Ask for evidence before a number.

A missing ingredient list or nutrition field should reduce confidence. Lychee can withhold a food score when information is insufficient. A long label alone is not proof of harm, and a short one does not resolve allergies or portion context.

Common questions

Before you scan.

How does Lychee score food?

Lychee uses a 0–100 food score that prioritizes processing. Additives, ingredient quality, sugar, salt, saturated fat, fiber and protein also matter. NOVA and Nutri-Score are inputs, not the entire score. Missing data can reduce confidence or prevent a score.

What if my product is missing from the database?

Try reading the ingredient label with Lychee. You can also submit or correct product data through its contribution flow. OCR and contributed records need checking; a missing ingredient list should be treated as incomplete data.
